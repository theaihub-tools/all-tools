Complete Date Calculation & Business Days Guide

Whether you are calculating notice periods, project milestones, legal filing limits, financial billing cycles, or simply counting down days to an event, date mathematics can become complicated due to varying month lengths and leap years. This tool handles Gregorian calendar calculations directly in your browser with zero server delay.

How to Use This Calculator

  1. Mode Selection: Use Date Difference to count time between two specific dates, or switch to Add / Subtract Days to project future deadlines.
  2. Pick Dates: Pick any calendar dates via the dropdown datepicker. The day of the week is automatically labeled beside each input.
  3. Presets: Use the one-click preset buttons (+7, +30, +90, End of Month, End of Year) for fast standard projections.
  4. Exclude Weekends: Toggle Business days only to remove Saturdays and Sundays from schedules.
  5. Save & Export: Copy the text summary directly to your clipboard, or click Add to Calendar (.ics) to generate a universal calendar import file.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How do I calculate days between two dates?

Select the "Date Difference" mode, choose your start date and end date, and click "Calculate". The tool will display the exact number of days, full calendar breakdown (years, months, days), weeks, business days, and total hours.

Can I add only working business days to a date?

Yes. Select the "Add / Subtract Days" tab, input your start date and number of days, and check the "Business days only" checkbox. Saturdays and Sundays will be skipped automatically.

Can I add weeks, months, or years together?

Yes. The tool features separate inputs for Years, Months, Weeks, and Days in the "Add / Subtract" mode. You can combine any combination of these units simultaneously.

How do I export this deadline into Google Calendar or Outlook?

After running your calculation, click the green "Add to Calendar (.ics)" button. A standard .ics file will download instantly, which you can double-click or import into Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, or Microsoft Outlook.

Does this tool account for leap years?

Yes. The calendar engine accurately accounts for February 29th during leap years across both historical and future calculations.
